**Abstract**

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory disease initiated by microbial infection that can lead to periodontal tissue damage and alveolar bone resorption. Macrophages contribute to the regulation of periodontal inflammation and local tissue homeostasis, but the role of integrin α9β1 in these processes remains unclear. Here, we combined analysis of human gingival single-cell transcriptomic data, a mouse model of myeloid cell-specific conditional Itga9 deletion, in vitro experiments with bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMMs), and conditioned-medium experiments in gingival fibroblasts to investigate how α9β1 affects the immune microenvironment and bone destruction in periodontitis. ITGA9 was highly expressed in human gingival macrophages and was further upregulated in periodontitis. Itga9 deletion altered the basal transcriptional state of BMMs and their response to Porphyromonas gingivalis (Pg), with reduced TNF production, downregulation of inflammation-related gene sets, and lower AKT and S6 phosphorylation following Pg stimulation. In vivo, myeloid Itga9 deletion reduced periodontal tissue damage and alveolar bone loss and was accompanied by changes in local immune cell composition and fibroblast-associated communication networks. Gene sets related to skeletal development, ossification, and extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interactions were positively enriched in fibroblasts. Further in vitro experiments showed that conditioned medium from Pg-stimulated KO BMMs was less inhibitory to gingival fibroblast mineralization than that from Pg-stimulated Het BMMs, as reflected by higher expression of mineralization-related genes, greater alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity, and increased mineralized nodule formation. These findings suggest that myeloid integrin α9β1 contributes to the regulation of immune and stromal cell functions in periodontitis and that targeting this receptor may help reduce inflammation-associated bone destruction and support the mineralization potential of gingival fibroblasts.

**Abstract**

RATIONALE: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is characterized by small airway disease (SAD) and emphysema, but mechanisms linking these pathologies remain unclear. SAD arises early and is the primary site of airflow limitation, marked by epithelial injury, mucus obstruction, and bronchiole loss. Excessive production of the mucin MUC5B by bronchiolar club cells is a key feature of SAD and is associated with impaired mucociliary clearance and inflammation. Club cells also function as progenitors for alveolar type II (ATII) cells, yet how their regenerative capacity is altered in COPD is unknown.
OBJECTIVES: To determine whether MUC5B overproduction in small airway club cells alters their capacity to regenerate ATII cells and contributes to emphysema progression.
METHODS: We assessed MUC5B expression in human emphysematous lungs and a porcine pancreatic elastase (PPE)-induced mouse model of emphysema. Genetic approaches were used to overexpress or conditionally delete Muc5b in club cells to evaluate effects on club-to-ATII differentiation, alveolar structure, and lung function in health and emphysema.
RESULTS: MUC5B is overexpressed in small airway club cells in human emphysematous lungs and animal models. Muc5b overexpression impairs differentiation of club cells into ATII cells and worsened lung function decline in emphysema. Importantly, conditional deletion of Muc5b during early disease stages restored club cell-derived alveolar regeneration.
CONCLUSIONS: MUC5B overproduction links SAD to defective alveolar repair by limiting club cell progenitor function. Targeting MUC5B may restore epithelial regeneration and slow early COPD progression.

### 3. ISM1 protects lung homeostasis via cell-surface GRP78-mediated alveolar macrophage apoptosis.

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America | 2022 Jan 25 | Terence Y W Lam, Ruowen Ge

- 来源：pubmed
- 槽位：领域经典
- 依据：abstract_only
- 第一作者/通讯作者单位：Department of Biological Sciences, Faculty of Science, National University of Singapore, Singapore 117543, Singapore.

**Abstract**

Alveolar macrophages (AMs) are critical for lung immune defense and homeostasis. They are orchestrators of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), with their number significantly increased and functions altered in COPD. However, it is unclear how AM number and function are controlled in a healthy lung and if changes in AMs without environmental assault are sufficient to trigger lung inflammation and COPD. We report here that absence of isthmin 1 (ISM1) in mice (Ism1-/- ) leads to increase in both AM number and functional heterogeneity, with enduring lung inflammation, progressive emphysema, and significant lung function decline, phenotypes similar to human COPD. We reveal that ISM1 is a lung resident anti-inflammatory protein that selectively triggers the apoptosis of AMs that harbor high levels of its receptor cell-surface GRP78 (csGRP78). csGRP78 is present at a heterogeneous level in the AMs of a healthy lung, but csGRP78high AMs are expanded in Ism1-/- mice, cigarette smoke (CS)-induced COPD mice, and human COPD lung, making these cells the prime targets of ISM1-mediated apoptosis. We show that csGRP78high AMs mostly express MMP-12, hence proinflammatory. Intratracheal delivery of recombinant ISM1 (rISM1) depleted csGRP78high AMs in both Ism1-/- and CS-induced COPD mice, blocked emphysema development, and preserved lung function. Consistently, ISM1 expression in human lungs positively correlates with AM apoptosis, suggesting similar function of ISM1-csGRP78 in human lungs. Our findings reveal that AM apoptosis regulation is an important physiological mechanism for maintaining lung homeostasis and demonstrate the potential of pulmonary-delivered rISM1 to target csGRP78 as a therapeutic strategy for COPD.

**Abstract**

Pulmonary fibrosis (PF) involves remodeling of lung architecture, and limited therapeutic options are available. Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1), an epigenetic regulator, has emerged as a promising antifibrotic target due to its role in apoptosis, inflammation, and chromatin remodeling. We evaluated the effects of a recombinant adenovirus expressing human SIRT1 (Ad-SIRT1) both in vitro and in vivo. **Abstract**

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is characterized by the sustained activation of interstitial fibroblasts leading to excessive collagen deposition and progressive organ failure. Epigenetic and metabolic abnormalities have been shown to contribute to the persistent activated state of scar-forming fibroblasts. However, how epigenetic changes regulate fibroblast metabolic responses to promote fibroblast activation and progressive fibrosis remains largely unknown. Here, we show that the epigenetic regulator CBX5 (chromobox protein homolog 5) is critical to the transition of quiescent fibroblasts to activated collagen-producing fibroblasts in response to bleomycin-induced lung injury. Loss of mesenchymal CBX5 attenuated fibrosis development, and this effect was accompanied by the downregulation of pathogenic fibroblast genes, including Cthrc1, Col1a1, and Spp1, and by the upregulation of metabolic genes with antifibrotic activity such as Ppara and Pparg. - 首次直接比较OA滑膜与SSc皮肤，定义出跨疾病保守的肌成纤维细胞样、炎症性成纤维细胞样和衬里样成纤维细胞亚群。
- 借助Xenium 5K空间转录组，证明这些保守成纤维细胞亚群在两种疾病中占据高度相似的ECM富集纤维化生态位和毗邻免疫细胞的炎症生态位。
- 发现两种疾病的转录重塑汇聚于共享功能通路和共同候选上游调控因子，并与IPF的纤维化程序及骨软骨程序部分重叠。

**研究设计**
- 比较OA滑膜与SSc皮肤组织，采用单细胞RNA-seq解析成纤维细胞活化状态。
- 使用Xenium 5K空间转录组定位疾病相关成纤维细胞亚群及其生态位。
- 跨疾病比较转录程序，并与IPF及骨软骨程序作参照。
- 推断共享功能通路和候选上游分子调控因子。

**主要发现**
- 鉴定出两病共有的疾病富集成纤维细胞亚群：纤维化性肌成纤维细胞样细胞、表达趋化因子和细胞因子的炎症性成纤维细胞及衬里样成纤维细胞。
- 这些亚群呈现趋同纤维化程序，并与IPF程序及骨软骨转录程序部分重叠。
- 空间上，肌成纤维细胞样细胞位于ECM富集的纤维化生态位，炎症性成纤维细胞位于毗邻浸润免疫细胞的炎症生态位。
- 两病的疾病相关转录重塑汇聚于共享通路和共同候选上游调控因子。

**为什么入选** 该预印本以跨疾病人组织单细胞图谱和空间转录组为核心，提出保守成纤维细胞状态、空间生态位和共享调控节点，对纤维化机制与基质-免疫互作研究有概念借鉴价值。
